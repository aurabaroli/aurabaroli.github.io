The Invisible Wall

By Aura Baroli

The following is an original article, written for the purpose of applying to Tra i Leoni.

We don’t exist the same way you do.

We live separated from the rest of society, kept to the sides by an invisible barrier which we are not able to overcome.

A glass panel which separates us, the animals, from you, the humans.

While this may appear like an excessively pessimistic description of the way in which transgender and non binary people exist with respect to the rest of society, given the remarkable achievements of the past decade, it is our reality. And what happened last February is a perfect example to showcase what I am referring to.

Just two weeks after I had sent my application to this University, on February 15th, every newspaper in this country broke the news about an incident that had occurred here, and reading about it made me ask myself if I had taken the right decision by choosing to come here.

Seven months ago, three students were suspended by the Disciplinary Committee after they were found to have violated the University Honor Code by posting transphobic comments on Instagram, underneath a post which announced the opening of new gender neutral bathrooms inside many campus buildings. Very briefly, they said they were going to use those bathrooms to have sex with those who were inside, i.e transgender and non-binary people; they even used an expression (“andare a trans”) which is usually employed, togheter with an even more vulgar one, by those men who are thinking about hiring a sex worker, thus linking trans people to sexual activity.

This connection isn’t casual: it is a natural consequence of the distorted perception of transgender and non binary people that is suspended in the air, all around us, like a deadly virus waiting to infect a brain: in those minds where the virus has settled, we exist as ridiculous, lurid, sexual, perverted, predatory, mentally ill creatures that must be either controlled or ostracized. The logical consequence of this belief is that, since we are disgusting, we can and should be treated as less worthy, less valuable individuals. This means that we can and should be made fun of, we can and should be harassed, we can and should be insulted, we can and should be used as if we were just pieces of meat that can be taken without consent and consumed for personal satisfaction. This is not to say that trans people who enjoy sex or sex workers who happen to be transgender contribute to the stereotypes people tend to associate with us, in fact no trans person is responsible for this distorted characterization of the members of our group. Most of what people know about transgender and non-binary doesn’t come from within the community, rather it is the product of those infected minds that think of us as lesser beings.

And if this isn’t the case, why are we still referred to as “un trans” or as “una trans”, as if we were part of a completely different species? Why do we get treated by medical and political institutions as if we were liars and manipulators? Why do people preemptively exclude us from their dating pool, as if we were sick subjects to avoid at all costs? Why do people kill us because of who we are? Why does the media strip us of our dignity by opting to use our deadname and the wrong pronouns every time one us is murdered or commits suicide, as if we were irrelevant and unworthy of remembrance?

Why do three university students say that they’re going to enter our bathrooms and use us, as if we were gadgets or toys?

Their comments are exactly what the wall needs in order to retain its strength in perpetuity: by leveraging our fear of being hurt, they succeed in keeping us away from the barrier, thus reinforcing the separation.

Fear is indeed a powerful weapon, one that is capable of keeping people on edge, restless, for countless days, until they are so tired of being alert that they simply step away from the world, only interacting with other trans and non binary people.

I’ve felt this way many times, especially after an unwanted encounter with an older man.

After they’ve spotted me, they find an excuse to start the conversation, like “When does the next bus arrive?” or “Do you need help with those groceries bags?”, which they immediately drop once it has served its purpose. They than ask for my phone number or want me to add theirs to my contacts, so that I’ll be able to call them if I ever need to.

Out of nowhere, they start telling me how beautiful I am and suggest I go to their place to do something. Up until this point, nothing in particular stands out as being particularly off-putting, except for the very little time it has taken then to go from small talk to sexual requests, but after I start saying no to their proposals, their tone and attitude changes quickly: they become increasingly insistent, repeatedly asking for my phone number and for me to go with them. It’s not an easy task to make them let me go, but eventually they do, their faces disappointed like that of a person who found a broken vending machine and tried to make the snacks fall from the shelves by banging on the glass.

Can transgender and cisgender people achieve coexistence when we can’t even share a street without us being afraid of getting harassed or hurt?

We can, though not by removing the wall, which should be left standing.

We can reach that goal not by creating a unified space, but rather by having you move to one that already exist: ours.

Those of you who are on the other side of the barrier should jump above it and land in our part of reality, leaving behind the wall everything you learned about us.

We would then able to exist the same way.
